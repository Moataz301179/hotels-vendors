## Slide 2: The Problem (Bank's Perspective)

### Why Egyptian banks don't have hotel corporate cards today:

| Barrier | Why it exists | What it costs you |
|---|---|---|
| **Seasonal cash flow** | Hotels earn in summer, pay year-round | Risk models flag them as "unstable" |
| **Informal financials** | Family-run hotels don't have audited books | Can't underwrite traditional credit |
| **30-day payment vs 90-day terms** | Hotels pay suppliers in 30 days, guests settle in 90 | Working capital mismatch |
| **20-30% credit card interest** | Existing products are consumer-grade, not B2B | No viable product to offer |
| **No transaction visibility** | Payments happen in cash, WhatsApp, or manual transfers | Zero data for relationship deepening |

**Result:** CIB (and every other Egyptian bank) has almost zero penetration into hospitality procurement spend.

## Appendix B: Risk Mitigation

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Hotel defaults on card | Authority Matrix prevents out-of-policy spend; credit limit tied to procurement history, not guesswork |
| Supplier fails to deliver | Delivery tracking + supplier rating system; factoring only for verified suppliers |
| Seasonal cash crunch | Dynamic credit limits adjust to seasonal curves; 90-day terms align with hotel revenue cycles |
| ETA compliance failure | Automated submission pipeline; dead-letter queue with manual resolution |
| Fraud / duplicate invoices | UUID-tagged invoices; blockchain-anchored audit trail |
