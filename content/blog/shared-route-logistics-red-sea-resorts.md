# Shared-Route Logistics: Solving Delivery Challenges for Red Sea Resorts

Delivering to remote resort destinations is expensive and unreliable. A truck traveling from Cairo to Hurghada (450km) might carry goods for only one hotel — driving up costs and delivery times.

HotelsVendors' shared-route logistics model, called **Shark-Breaker**, solves this by consolidating deliveries across multiple hotels on the same route.

## The Problem

Hotels in Red Sea and South Sinai face unique logistics challenges:

- **Distance**: 400–600km from major distribution centers in Cairo and Alexandria
- **Low density**: Hotels spread along coastal corridors with few nearby alternatives
- **Seasonal variance**: Delivery volume spikes 5x during high season (October–April)
- **Unreliable carriers**: Traditional logistics providers offer inconsistent service and pricing

The result: delivery costs can represent 15–25% of product cost — compared to 5–8% in urban areas.

## How Shark-Breaker Works

### Hub-and-Spoke Model

HotelsVendors operates regional hubs in:
- **Cairo** (serving mainland routes)
- **Alexandria** (serving North Coast/Marsa Matruh)
- **Hurghada** (serving Red Sea resorts)
- **Sharm El-Sheikh** (serving South Sinai)

### Route Optimization

When multiple hotels on the platform order from the same supplier or distribution corridor, the AI agent:

1. **Clusters orders** by destination and delivery window
2. **Optimizes truck loads** to maximize vehicle utilization
3. **Schedules consolidated deliveries** on shared routes
4. **Tracks in real-time** with GPS and delivery confirmation

## Real-World Example

A group of 12 hotels along the Hurghada–Safaga corridor:

- **Before**: Each hotel arranged own logistics. Average delivery cost: EGP 2,400 per order. Frequency: 3x/week per hotel.
- **After Shark-Breaker**: Consolidated to 2 shared deliveries/week for the entire corridor. Average delivery cost: EGP 1,440 per order. Savings: EGP 960 per order × 36 orders/week = EGP 34,560/week across the group.
